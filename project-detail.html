The Brief

A Home That Moves
With the Sun

The client came to us with a single, clear aspiration: to live in a home that felt different at every hour of the day. Not through decoration or furniture, but through architecture itself — through the way light entered, moved, and changed.

The site in South Delhi presented both constraint and opportunity: a narrow plot facing east-west, with a mature neem tree that the family refused to remove. We accepted both as gifts. The neem became a courtyard anchor. The east-west orientation became the engine of our design.

Our response was a 6,200 square foot residence built around a central double-height void — a light well — that pulls morning sun deep into the heart of the house, softens it through a brise-soleil through the day, and releases it as warm amber through western glass at dusk.

"We don't design rooms. We design the experience of being in a room at 7am, at noon, and at sunset. Architecture is time made visible."

— Ar. Shailesh Kumar

Chapter 03

Materials Chosen
with Intention

Board-Formed Concrete

Cast in-situ with reclaimed timber formwork, leaving the wood grain imprinted on every exposed surface. Raw, honest, permanent.

Source: Local RMC, Delhi NCR

Low-Iron Float Glass

Ultra-clear glass with minimal green tint, allowing true colour rendering of the landscape and sky beyond. Double-glazed for thermal performance.

Source: Saint-Gobain, India

Mild Steel (Patinated)

Structural steel left to develop a natural rust patina over two seasons, then sealed. The warm orange-brown creates dialogue with the concrete grey.

Source: SAIL, Processed locally

Chapter 05

The Space, Complete

"When the client moved in, they called to say the light at 7am through the east window hit the concrete wall exactly the way we had drawn it two years before. That phone call is why we do this work."

Chapter 06

Life Inside the Light House

The family of four reports that they no longer need clocks to know the time of day. The light tells them. Morning is east and blue. Noon is vertical and sharp. Evening is amber and long-shadowed.

Architecture, at its best, is not decoration. It is orientation. It is the daily reminder that you are a creature of this Earth, moving through light and time.
